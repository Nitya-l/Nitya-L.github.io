# Ultrasound Training Phantom for Jugular Vein Cannulation
 
**Term Project** · Fluid and Materials Lab, University of Washington <br>
**October 2023 – December 2023**

---
 
## Background
 
Central venous access via jugular vein cannulation is a common but technically demanding clinical procedure. It is typically performed under ultrasound guidance and requires practitioners to simultaneously interpret real-time imaging, navigate complex anatomy, and execute precise needle placement. Inexperienced users often face a steep learning curve and opportunities to practice on real patients are limited as the procedure is risky.
 
Physical training phantoms are models that simulate tissue anatomy and allow practitioners to practice procedures in a controlled setting. As such, they offer a way to build procedural skill before working with patients. This project involved designing and building a low-cost phantom that realistically simulates the neck anatomy relevant to jugular vein cannulation and is compatible with standard ultrasound imaging equipment.
 
---
 
## My Contributions
 
**Phantom fabrication**: Developed a ballistic gel-based model of the neck that mimics the acoustic properties of soft tissue and bone under an ultrasound, enabling realistic image quality during simulated procedures. Embedded anatomically positioned structures to represent the jugular vein and surrounding anatomy.
 
**Active fluid flow system**: Designed and integrated a system for active fluid flow through the simulated vessel, providing realistic dynamic feedback during needle insertion that is visible under an ultrasound scan. This model was closer to the physiological conditions of an actual procedure than static models.
 
**Ultrasound compatibility**: Validated the phantom with a linear array ultrasound probe to confirm image quality and ensure the model was compatible with standard clinical imaging equipment used in training settings.

## My Takeaways 
While this was a short term team project, it was one of the most technically ambitious course projects I worked on as an undergraduate student. It required integrating principles of fluid mechanics, material science, and ultrasound imaging with a clear understanding of the clinical requirements of central venous access procedures. As the team member responsible for the physical model, I gained experience translating anatomical and physiological constraints into a functional prototype, including material selection, fabrication, and iterative testing under ultrasound imaging conditions.
