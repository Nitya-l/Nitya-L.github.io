## Background
 
The meniscus is a fibrocartilaginous tissue in the knee joint that plays a critical role in load distribution, shock absorption, and joint stability. It exhibits viscoelastic mechanical behavior which means that it is able to dissipate energy over time rather than hold on to energy, as an elastic material would. Damage or degeneration of the meniscus is a major contributor to osteoarthritis and meniscal tears occur frequently in athletes. Treatment options for these conditions are invasive and fail to restore the full capabilities of the native meniscus. One goal of the T.A.R.E lab is to create a model of the meniscus that could serve as a platform to develop less invasive therapies for OA and meniscal tears. 
 
Additionally, mechanical cues at the tissue level influence cell behavior and regeneration. The viscoleastic properties of the meniscus play a major role in meniscal cell differentiation and maturation but most models utilize elastic hydrogel scaffolds. Hydrogels are a promising platform for studying this kind of mechanobiology because their physical properties in the form of stiffness, viscosity, and crosslinking density can be precisely tuned to mimic biological tissues. This project focused on engineering a hyaluronic acid-based hydrogel system that recapitulates the native viscoelastic properties of the meniscus, with the long-term goal of understanding how those properties influence meniscal cell mechanotransduction.

## My Contributions
 
**Hydrogel system development**: Developed and optimized a hyaluronic acid-based hydrogel formulation capable of mimicking the biomechanical properties of native meniscal tissue. This involved iterating on crosslinker concentrations and polymer modifications to achieve targeted viscoelastic profiles. 
 
**Mechanical characterization**: Performed and optimized swelling studies, uniaxial compression tests, and stress relaxation experiments using a dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA) system. Stress relaxation behavior was a key readout for assessing viscoelasticity as how quickly the material dissipates stress under constant strain can represent viscoelastic behavior. Shear tests were also performed using a rheometer where the key readout was Tau, the ratio between the storage and loss moduli. 
 
**Data analysis pipeline**: Developed MATLAB code to automatically process raw output from the DMA testing equipment into interpretable mechanical parameters, and to compare different curve-fitting and analysis methods across experimental conditions. This not only reduced the time required to analyze data by 75% but also allowed for easier manipulation of analysis techniques. 
 
**Mentorship**: Trained an undergraduate student in hydrogel fabrication protocols and safe operation of the DMA testing equipment.
