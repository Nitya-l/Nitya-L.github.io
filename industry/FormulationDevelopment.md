# Investigating Factors Affecting Protein Stability and Viscosity
 
**Formulation Development and Drug Product Intern** · Lundbeck Seattle Biopharmaceuticals, Bothell, WA <br>
**June 2023 – September 2023**

---
 
## Background
 
Developing a stable, injectable protein drug product requires careful design considerations. High-concentration protein formulations, such as those used in subcutaneous injections, need to remain stable over long shelf lives while also being viscous enough to stay in solution but not so viscous that they're difficult or painful to inject. Formulation variables such as pH, buffer composition, excipient choice, temperature and their effects on viscosity and protein-protein interactions are not always intuitive but are important to validate.
 
This project investigated how a combination of formulation parameters affected the viscosity and stability of a protein drug product candidate, with the goal of identifying the conditions most likely to support a viable, manufacturable formulation. A major goal of this project was to also characterize the capabilities of a new high-throughput stability screening platfom, the UNCLE, and develop a standard operating procedure for future use. 
 
---
 
## My Contributions
 
**Experimental design and execution**
Designed and executed a multi-variable study examining the effects of buffer pH, additive type and concentration, and temperature on product viscosity and protein-protein interactions. Used industry-standard instrumentation and adhered to general manufacturing practices throughout.
 
**Statistical analysis**
Analyzed experimental data in JMP statistical software to assess the relative impact of each variable on formulation performance, identify interaction effects, and determine which conditions best supported product stability and injectability.
 
**Communication**
Delivered a 20-minute oral presentation to a broad scientific audience at a sitewide meeting, summarizing the project rationale, key findings, and actionable recommendations for formulation optimization.

## My Takeaways 
This internship was my first exposure to industry drug product development and reshaped how I think about translational science. Through coursework, I had primarily focused on early-stage discovery and innovation, without fully appreciating how many variables influence a product’s ability to reach patients. One key aspect of the product I was working with was reducing patient burden by lowering injection frequency for migraine treatment. This made me consider formulation design not only in terms of molecular performance, but also usability, manufacturability, and patient adherence, as well as how sensitive protein therapeutics are to small changes in formulation conditions. Beyond technical skills, I learned the importance of working in a highly regulated environment and how to create operating procedures that create reproducible results. Overall, this experience broadened my understanding of how the biopharmaceutical industry operates.
